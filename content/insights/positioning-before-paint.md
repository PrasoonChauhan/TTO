When growth slows, founders often reach for a rebrand. New logo, new colours, new website. Six weeks later the company looks better and sells exactly the same.

The problem was never the paint.

## Identity expresses a story. It cannot invent one.

A brand identity is a set of signals: type, colour, imagery, tone. Signals only work when there is a clear message to amplify. Without one, a beautiful identity just makes the confusion look expensive.

> If you cannot say what makes you different, no typeface will say it for you.

## Write the sentence first

Before any design work, we ask founders to complete one sentence:

**For** [specific customer] **who** [specific problem], **we are the** [category] **that** [single biggest difference], **unlike** [the real alternative].

It feels simple. It is not. Most teams need several drafts, and the arguments along the way are the most valuable part of the project.

### What good looks like

- **Specific customer:** "Finance teams at 50 to 500 person SaaS companies", not "businesses".
- **Real alternative:** usually a spreadsheet, an agency or doing nothing, not your closest competitor.
- **One difference:** the thing a customer would miss most if you disappeared.

## Test it outside the building

Read the sentence to five customers and five prospects. Ask them to repeat it back in their own words. If their version is sharper than yours, use theirs.

## Then brief the designer

Now the identity has a job: make that sentence feel true at a glance. Every choice can be judged against it. Does this colour feel like the category we want to own? Does this tone sound like the customer we serve?

Strategy first, then design. In that order, a rebrand pays for itself.
