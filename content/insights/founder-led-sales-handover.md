Founders sell with conviction no hire can match. They know the product, the vision and every objection by heart. That is why founder-led sales works so well, and why it eventually stops working.

## The signs it is time

- Deals wait for the founder's calendar.
- Win rates drop whenever someone else runs the call.
- The founder spends more time selling than building.

If two of these are true, the handover is already late.

## Step 1: Capture the story

Before you hire, document what the founder actually says. Record ten sales calls. Pull out the opening, the questions that qualify a deal, the three proof points that land, and the answers to the five most common objections.

> You cannot hand over a process that only exists in one person's head.

## Step 2: Hire to run, not to invent

Your first seller should execute a proven story, not design a new one. Look for someone who has joined an early team before and is excited by structure, not frightened of it.

## Step 3: Hand over by stage

Do not hand over everything at once. Move one stage at a time:

1. **Qualification first.** The new seller runs first calls; the founder joins the second.
2. **Demos next.** The seller runs the demo; the founder handles pricing.
3. **Closing last.** The founder steps back to executive sponsor on large deals only.

Each step takes four to eight weeks. Measure win rates at every stage so you know when to move on.

## Step 4: Make the founder a multiplier

The goal is not for founders to stop selling. It is for them to sell where it matters most: strategic accounts, partnerships and fundraising. Everything else should run without them.
