Most early-stage content fails for the same reason: volume without a point. Two posts a week, none of them the best answer to anything.

There is a better way, and it fits a team of two.

## Start from questions, not topics

Your sales calls are a goldmine. List every question prospects ask before they buy. Those questions are your editorial calendar. Each one deserves a page that answers it completely.

> The best marketing content is the answer your best salesperson gives, written down.

## One piece, five channels

Write one excellent article a fortnight. Then turn it into:

- a LinkedIn post from the founder,
- a short email to your list,
- three social snippets with the strongest lines,
- a slide for the sales deck,
- an answer the team can paste into prospect emails.

One piece of thinking, five places it works.

## Measure what compounds

Ignore vanity numbers. Track three things per article:

1. **Organic visits after 90 days.** Good content grows; weak content fades.
2. **Assisted conversions.** How many demo requests read it first?
3. **Sales usage.** How often does the team send it to prospects?

Kill or rewrite anything that scores zero on all three after six months.

## The compounding effect

A fortnightly article for a year is 26 pieces, each answering a real buyer question. By year two, they bring in more demand than anything you could buy, and they keep working while you sleep.
